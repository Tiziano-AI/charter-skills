# Where a product's character comes from

A product's character comes through what it opens on, what it decides for the
person, what it calls things, how it paces repeated work, where it spends
emphasis and what the person carries away. Look for the particulars that
change those decisions. Brand adjectives such as "warm", "bold" or "calm" can
describe their expression, but do not establish which handling of the work
fits. Some choices should differ from a sibling product; others will rightly
be shared.

## Reading a practice

Watch, or ask about, the last real occasion rather than the typical user:

- What the person holds together, compares, changes and keeps. A photographer
  selecting a set compares many frames at once; an archivist retrieving a known
  original needs exact indexing. The unit of work decides the main surface.
- Who else is present or receives the result: a room of friends, a kitchen
  line, a family, a client who will see the output.
- What the current alternative does well enough that people tolerate its pain:
  paper is glanceable and needs no login, a group chat already has everyone in
  it, a spreadsheet lets anyone add a column.
- The words they use when nobody is designing for them.
- The rhythm: how often, for how long, under what pressure, and what happens
  between sessions.
- The moment they would show someone else, or describe afterwards.

Let a finding change the design where it changes the work. If another practice
could use the same screens after a noun swap, check whether an important
difference has been missed. A shared structure that serves both practices is a
valid result of that check.

## Worked translations

Each case shows a practice, the decisions it implies, and a nearby practice in
which the same decisions would be wrong. They are constructed illustrations,
not research findings; the point is the move from particulars to decisions.

### The pass in a busy kitchen

During service the expeditor calls tickets, cooks read their station's orders
from a metre away with wet or gloved hands, and seconds matter. Paper tickets
hang on a rail in the order they arrived, so a ticket's position shows its age.
The words are the kitchen's: fire, 86, all day, on the fly.

The screen during service is the rail, and nothing else competes with it. Item
names read at arm's length, modifiers and allergies sit apart where nobody can
miss them, and each ticket shows how long it has waited, changing as it ages. A
ticket clears with one large target or a bump bar when completion only changes
recoverable queue state. Keep fulfilled tickets reachable so a mistaken bump
can be recalled without interrupting the next order. If the action also fires
work, prints a ticket or sends a message, handle those effects separately;
recalling the display does not undo them. The station's all-day counts sit on
the same screen. The character is precision under pressure; menu editing and
reports stay ordinary forms used at a desk.

The same restaurant's reservation book, used by a host between greetings, is
read up close and needs the shape of the night (covers per slot, tables
turning), not a rail.

### A fantasy-football league among friends in Italy

A *lega* of friends meets once a year for the *asta*, an evening auction, often
in someone's living room, where each manager bids from a fixed budget of
credits. Every *giornata* each manager sets a *formazione* before the round's
first match, and the players' ratings (*voti*) with their *bonus* and *malus*
decide the score. The league writes its own rules before the auction. The group
chat argues all week.

The auction gets a room mode: a shared screen with the player on the block, the
current bid and every manager's remaining credits, with bids placed from
phones. The lineup screen opens on the formation and the players available
this round, with the injured and suspended marked before the deadline. The
league's rules are set in one place by whoever runs the league, visible to
everyone; they are the league's identity, not each manager's preference.
Managers who choose a lineup-lock reminder receive it against their league's
actual deadline. Emphasis goes to auction night and the moment a round's
results land and the table moves. The interface speaks Italian football's
words; a league in another language would use its own.

A national prediction contest among thousands of strangers has no room and no
auction; one fixed scoring system serves everyone, and its peak is elsewhere.

### Closing the affairs of someone who has died

A family member, often doing it for the first time, notifies banks, a pension
provider, utilities and insurers, gathers certified copies of the death
certificate, and waits for replies over months, in bursts, between grief. The
alternative is a folder of letters and a list on paper.

The product opens on one plain view: what is done, what is waiting on whom and
since when, and what comes next. Each institution's requirements are stated
before the person gathers documents (which needs a certified copy, which
accepts a scan). It resumes on the case's current state after weeks away.
Reminders concern a deadline the person set or an institution imposes, an
agreed next step or a reply needing attention; silence fits when there is
nothing useful to tell. Status words state the case exactly, such as "Closed"
or "Waiting for reply since 3 March", without treating absence as lost
progress. Emphasis goes to the calm of that one view and to a final record,
printable, that everything is closed, which the family can keep.

A probate professional handling dozens of estates needs a dense queue across
cases and batch letters; there, calm means efficiency.

### Preparing a DJ set

A DJ digs for tracks, sorts them into crates by feel, sets cue points, checks
which tracks mix by tempo and key, then plays live, where the set bends to the
room and there is no time to look anything up. Judgment is by ear.

First use points at the folder the DJ already has; analysis runs while they
listen, and the first thing they hear is two of their own tracks blended at a
matched tempo. Previews start from the cue point, not from the first second.
Tracks that would mix next appear beside the crate, by tempo and key, rather
than behind a separate assistant. Emphasis goes to hearing a transition before
committing to it. The ending is an export to the USB stick in the venue's
actual player format, with the required playlists and cue points checked on a
compatible player before the gig. Retain what was actually played when that
record is available.

A radio music scheduler must inspect the broadcast log against rotation,
separation and timed-break constraints. That may call for a timed log with
scheduling feedback rather than the DJ's auditioning workspace; choose the
working surface from the actual scheduling decisions.

### Collecting K-pop photocards

For a release with randomized member photocards across versions, collectors
may organize their pulls in binders and trade duplicates, across countries by
post. Look at this group's actual collection lists and exchanges for its terms
and release rhythm.

The empty binder shows the album's set as outlines by member and version, so
the first pull fills a real gap. Trade matching starts from the collector's
gaps and duplicates. The count that matters is the collector's own, such as a
complete set of their bias, the member they follow most closely, not a badge
the product invents. If this collector
group's own posts and exchanges use a loud, playful, emoji-heavy voice, the
product can carry it into discovery and celebration. State condition, postage
and who pays precisely; another group's voice may be different.

A dealer selling graded cards needs condition, provenance and price, in a
shop's voice.

### Logging contacts in a radio contest

In the CQ World Wide DX Contest, an operator logs callsigns and exchanges
during the contest period. Duplicate contacts on a band add no points but
remain in the log; zones and countries affect multipliers. Scoring and
required fields come from this contest's rules, not from a generic logging
template.

Make the normal logging path completable from the keyboard, with immediate
feedback on whether a callsign is a dupe or a new mult, while retaining
correction and alternative input. The screen stays legible to tired eyes in a
dark room. Keep entry distinct from submitting the log. Emphasis goes to rapid,
legible entry without losing accuracy; the ending leaves a valid Cabrillo file
before the deadline, with submission status distinct from a local save.

A casual operator's logbook of long conversations is leisurely: notes, photos
of exchanged cards, a map of contacts.
