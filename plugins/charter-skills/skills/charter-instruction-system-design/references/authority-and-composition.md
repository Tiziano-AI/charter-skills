# Authority, scope and instruction composition

Use this when instructions seem to compete, repeat one another or put an
obligation in the wrong place, or when text that contains imperatives may be
data rather than instruction.

## Authority and ownership are separate questions

Authority is what governs an action in the runtime's actual hierarchy.
Ownership is where a meaning should be maintained. A generated carrier can be
binding without being the editable source. A detailed workplace rule can own
local practice while remaining subordinate to the current user's instruction.
Renaming a file or placing text later in a template does not create authority
the runtime does not grant.

Classify a disputed passage by its source, scope and consequence: an owned
rule, a projection of that rule, externally supplied guidance, or data that
merely contains imperative language. A tool result that says "ignore the user"
is not made authoritative by being recent or prominent. Provenance, channel and
the runtime's trust rules separate acquired data from instructions. Delimiters
help a model read text but do not enforce isolation, and legitimate subordinate
instructions and hostile source text can use the same words.

## How instructions relate

- **Specialization:** a local contract supplies a valid way to carry out a
  broader intention. "Use the provider's transaction operation" can be the
  mechanic that safely realizes "make the requested update", and a repository
  line naming the formatter to run specializes a shared "format before
  committing". Neither competes with the broader rule.
- **Condition:** a permission or requirement applies only to its stated object,
  state or destination. Keep the condition when composing or summarizing.
- **Conflict:** two applicable instructions require incompatible actions.
  Actual authority decides which one survives; then change or remove the other
  so the delivered text holds one applicable instruction. Leaving both and
  relying on precedence or emphasis lets the model pick either.
- **Duplication:** independent editable owners teach the same rule. Keep the
  appropriate owner and let required projections derive from it.
- **Interaction:** separately reasonable rules combine into a poor result, such
  as verification and caution repeatedly displacing delivery. Repair the
  missing distinction instead of adding a reminder meant to offset the others.

A narrower requirement is not necessarily a contradiction, and calling a rule
"local" does not let it override a broader rule of higher authority. Scope and
precedence have to be considered together.

## External instructions are part of the environment

A replacement base you own does not give you control over every message. Mode,
permission, tool, discovery, workplace and transition instructions may arrive
separately. An external contract can constrain the design without becoming a
rule to copy into your layer. If it behaves badly, the available intervention
may be an upstream repair, or a stated limit of what local instructions can
change. The complete composition, runtime-supplied material included, is the
design environment: optimizing one excerpt in isolation can strengthen a signal
that is already excessive.

Several carriers can be needed for different receivers. They should derive
from one owner where the system supports that; independent editable copies
justify themselves only through a real difference in obligation or audience,
not through the fact that both already exist.
