# Commitment, reversibility and learning

An option's value depends partly on the future choices it preserves.
Reversibility is not binary: a contract may be cancellable while data
migration, team skills, customer expectations and integration residue remain.

The relevant map distinguishes irreversible commitment, recoverable commitment
and recovery time, switching cost, dependencies created or foreclosed, knowledge
available only through action, and the deadline after which the option decays.
These dimensions explain why a seemingly cheap trial can be a costly commitment,
or why paying more initially can buy valuable flexibility.

## When a staged choice buys an option

A pilot helps when the full decision depends on an uncertainty that small-scale
use can expose. The pilot needs to preserve a real ability to choose differently:
limited integration, bounded external commitments, and a result available before
delay destroys the opportunity.

An implementation pilot cannot resolve demand if real customers never see it.
A demand pilot may not establish unit economics if service is supplied manually.
The stage is informative only for the mechanism it exercises; "pilot succeeded"
is not a portable finding.

Success, failure and stop conditions can clarify which findings change the
next commitment. They are useful decision boundaries, not ceremonial metrics.
Where the response is already clear from natural use, a formal pilot may add
little.

## Learning can be worth less than delay

Waiting is attractive when useful information arrives without much cost and
the option remains available. Acting is attractive when delay is expensive,
the choice is reversible, or information is available only through use.
The best staged option trades those effects, rather than treating caution as
automatically superior.

An observable revisit trigger links new evidence to a changed premise:
reopen when a contract, demand measure, failure rate or dependency changes enough
to cross the choice's switching boundary. The threshold comes from the decision
model or its owner. A date alone is meaningful only when it changes a real
opportunity, obligation or information state.
