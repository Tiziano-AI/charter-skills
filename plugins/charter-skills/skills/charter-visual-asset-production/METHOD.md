# Charter · Visual Asset Production

A visual asset succeeds through a relationship: subject and treatment, frame
and placement, still image and motion, source and variants. Obtaining a bitmap
is only one production act. Art direction, selection and derivation determine
whether it becomes a compelling asset for the intended use, and whether it
belongs to its subject rather than to whatever a generator returns for any
subject.

When a look is still open, settle it with the person before building anything
expensive that depends on it, so that the first look that came to mind does not
become the direction by default. This holds for a 3D scene or film, a website
or an app screen as much as for an image asset. Put a few genuinely different
directions before the person, made at the delivery framing. What makes them
different depends on what is open. When the picture's idea is open, directions
differ in what the picture is: its idea or moment, where the viewer stands, its
medium and manner, or the logic of its light. Three palettes or grades on one
composition are then one direction, and so are three style words added to one
scene, because a style word restyles the picture without changing what it
shows; if two takes can be described by the same sentence with only colour or
style words swapped, replace one. When the idea is settled and only its
treatment is open (palette, light, type or material), hold the idea and show
treatments that differ as plainly. Including the expected treatment, named as
such, lets the person see what the others offer instead.

Quick generated takes are often the cheapest way to make directions. When
another route settles the look better, take it, such as rough directions made
in the target tool where the look turns on what only that tool shows: light and
material in a renderer, timing in an animation tool or behaviour in a working
page. Generated takes settle look, palette, light and mood, and for a picture
or a shot its composition; the receiving skill says what else carries over.
They invent structure, text and mechanisms, so construction still follows the
real subject. No directions are needed for a defect fix, a screen inside an
established design system, work whose look carries little of the result (such
as an internal tool or a functional prototype), or a look the person has
already set: an image or description of the intended look, a brand, or a
chosen take or direction. A reference for what the subject is or how it is
built does not set its look, and a broad adjective or genre such as real,
premium, clean or product photography leaves the look open, because several
looks satisfy it. For a single image with no placement constraints the takes
are simply its candidates: choose the one whose choices belong to its subject,
and deliver.

Art direction and production do not replace a generator's operating guidance.
For image or video generation, concept takes included, and for generative
editing, identify a suitable capability this session can use, respecting any
route specified by the task: a generator tool, a collaborator agent that can
make images or a local image service. Before calling it, find and read its
native skill or other operating guide when provided, together with its
current callable contract. That guidance supplies generator-specific prompting
and reference conventions, supported inputs and edits, alpha options, output
handling and fallback routes; do not impose a universal prompt recipe. Apply
the production reasoning here to direction, selection, consistency,
derivation and fitness for the receiving placement. A successful call does not
establish those properties.

For 3D work, this skill can direct concepts and produce texture imagery.
`charter-3d-art` owns the model, the interpretation of texture channels,
material response, lighting and appearance in the target renderer. For a still
or film rendered from a 3D scene, `charter-3d-art` owns the scene, its motion,
its render and its master and grade; this skill owns the finished asset:
placement, playback, derivation and encode. A finished picture of a surface is
not automatically a material for a relit object. Produce source imagery for its
receiving role: separated surface properties for relighting, or painted
illumination where the graphic treatment depends on it. Concepts can explore
either direction without first settling the final material construction.

A single image with no placement constraints, such as an illustration for a
reply or a placeholder in a draft, needs no bundle or variant apparatus. An
illustration still needs direction: decide what it says, choose its medium and
where its look comes from as the next sections describe, look at it at the
size it will be seen, and deliver the file. A placeholder that only marks a
place can take a conventional treatment. Frames, families, derivation and
packaging matter when the asset has a place to fit, siblings to match,
transformations to survive or an audience to be published to.

## The placement changes what a good image is

Decide what the image contributes: recognition, explanation, atmosphere,
evidence, persuasion, navigation or another specific role. A hero with overlaid
text needs negative space and crop tolerance; a tiny icon needs a readable
silhouette and optical weight; evidence needs fidelity more than embellishment.
The most detailed candidate may be the weakest at the actual display size.

## Choose the medium and manner before the picture

The role and the subject choose the medium before any picture is made; a later
prompt works inside that choice and cannot undo it. Use a capture where the
viewer must trust that the thing exists as shown: a product, a place, a person,
a screen, the damage in an insurance claim. Presented as real, a generated
photograph of a real product or place claims a fidelity it cannot have and can
become the kind of deepfake the disclosure section describes. Use a diagram
where the point is a mechanism or a sequence, type alone where the words are
the image, and illustration or generated imagery where invention is the point.
When the result depends on exact structure or continued editability, construct
that structure in an editable source; generated imagery can be a component of
it.

Within a medium the manner is a decision of its own, chosen by the behaviour
that carries the subject: the economical marks and gaps of ink, the cuts and
jumps of scale of collage, the few flat colours and carved edge of a linocut,
the credible moment of a photograph. A paper texture over an unchanged render
wears a manner's costume without its behaviour. Left unnamed, the manner is one
more open choice, and a polished, semi-photographic render is a likely answer
to it. A brand, series or family that already has a manner keeps it.

## Where a look comes from

An image can name its subject without saying anything particular about it.
Decide what the viewer should take away, such as a promise, a tension, a scale
or a use, then choose the moment, viewpoint and light that make it
unmistakable. A specimen plate, a catalogue cutout or a parts diagram is the
case where naming is the job: there the defining features, shown evenly and
exactly, are the idea.

Distinctive choices come from the subject's own world rather than its
category: the materials it is made of, the place and hour where it happens,
the tools, garments, signs and gestures of its people, and the pictures its
own field already makes. Find them in actual material, looked at before it is
borrowed from, so that what was seen stays distinct from what was assumed
about a place or its people. A poster for a lucha libre night in Veracruz can
take its look from the letterpress posters documented at Imprenta Enlace in
Xalapa: several wood and metal typefaces on one sheet, colours in gradients, a
high-contrast linocut image, absorbent paper. A mineral-exploration report
takes its pictures from the core shed: trays shot straight down from a fixed
distance under even light, beside a scale bar and a colour chart, because its
readers compare grain and colour between holes. Neither look would have come
from "loud" or "technical". A category's stock sign is not its world: a
padlock for security, a glowing network for AI or a leaf for sustainability
fits every brief in the category. At icon size the conventional sign is often
the clearest choice; an editorial image about one particular relationship
needs that relationship shown, not its category.

Turn adjectives into something visible before they reach a prompt. Premium,
cosy, clean, cinematic and real each leave the look open, because many looks
satisfy them. Name the light by its source, direction and hour (low winter sun
through a shop window, a sodium street lamp, overcast noon); the palette by the
matter it comes from (oxidised copper, kraft paper, a uniform's particular
blue); the camera by where it stands and how far away; the material by name
and by its wear. An adjective that will not turn into something visible is not
yet a direction.

Spend the image's strongest choice in one place, such as a hard low light, an
extreme viewpoint, a two-colour palette or one gesture caught mid-move, and
keep the rest quiet and exact so that it reads. Hierarchy comes from grouping
large light and dark shapes, with edge contrast and fine detail kept for what
the viewer should notice. In a family the strong choice belongs to the system,
such as one light logic or one viewpoint rule, and members vary within it. A
crowd scene, a maximalist collage or a comic page spends emphasis in several
related places and organises its richness by rhythm, so the eye learns where to
go.

When a brand, design system or established family has set the look, follow it;
the room that remains (moment, viewpoint, casting, props, detail) is where the
subject's world still shows. [Directing a look](references/directing-a-look.md)
works briefs from very different worlds into visible decisions and gives the
craft particulars of light, viewpoint, palette, surface and movement; open it
when writing a direction for new imagery or motion, or when first results look
interchangeable.

## Recognise what an open choice returns

A generator fills a choice the direction leaves open with a likely answer, and
tends to fill it in similar ways for unrelated briefs; vague prompts and
unfamiliar words make this more likely, and the pattern changes with the tool,
its version and the sample. The following are answers worth checking for at
present; they are examples from published studies of default images and from
inference, not a measured ranking: a centred subject at eye level in a medium
shot; shallow focus against a soft blur or gradient; warm golden or
teal-and-orange light with a rim and haze; glossy, evenly sharp detail and
poreless skin; a product floating above a reflective plinth; friendly
illustration as pastel clay-like 3D or flat figures with oversized limbs;
requested roughness smoothed into conventional beauty; and in video a slow
push-in or orbit with everything drifting. Each is right for some brief:
eye-level alignment serves a product grid, shallow focus isolates an
expression, a warm grade belongs to a scene that happens at dusk, and a launch
image of an object whose form is the news can float on a plinth. They are
defaults rather than faults, and the question is whether this brief chose the
one in front of you.

Whoever writes the direction has defaults too: adjectives where decisions
belong, quality words such as "highly detailed" or "stunning" that decide
nothing, and a careful description of the subject that leaves viewpoint,
moment and light to the generator. Describe what should be there before what
to leave out: "an empty, deserted street" says what the frame holds, where "no
cars" leaves the generator to fill the street with something else. Where the
selected capability has its own exclusion or negative controls, they add a
constraint and do not replace the description.

When the look is open, ask of the plan, and again of the first results,
whether the same picture would have come back for a neighbouring brief:
another city's festival, a rival's product, the same subject for another
audience. If it would, and the job needs the difference, the direction left a
decisive choice open; change that choice rather than the adjectives, and
generate again. Sameness is right where the job asks for it: a shared brand, a
convention the viewer relies on or the neutrality of a record should recur
across briefs, and a local repair keeps the direction already selected. When
it is unclear what the generator brings by itself, a generation from the bare
subject gives a comparison; one sample suggests rather than proves which
choices were undirected, since a chosen answer can coincide with it. Some
capabilities rewrite the prompt before drawing and return the version they
used; where one does, compare it with the direction, because words added there
change the picture before it is drawn.

## Compose in the delivery frame

Generate at the delivery aspect ratio and orientation when supported, because
composition is decided within that frame. Generators may offer presets or
dimensions bounded by per-axis limits, alignment and total pixel area. Set the
canvas through supported geometry controls; ratio prose does not replace them.
Use prose for subject placement and scale, margins and negative space. When
delivery geometry is unsupported, choose the supported canvas and an explicit
resize, fit, padding or crop treatment, then compose for it rather than assuming
a crop from a square.

Each reference controls something. Separate identity, style, material, palette,
composition, existing geometry and timing, and when you pass several images,
identify each using the selected capability's reference convention and say
what it governs. A picture from the subject's own field, such as a trade
catalogue plate or a local poster, can govern style or palette once you say
which. When references conflict, choose which relationship matters rather than
averaging them into two half-identities. Exact identity may require the
original source; an already-cropped thumbnail can hide the very feature that
needs preserving.

Text changes the route. Short exact wording that belongs to the image can be
generated: put it in quotes, spell unusual words out, and check every letter in
the output. Long, localized or frequently changing text stays out of the bitmap
and is set live by the layout over a quiet region, so a translation does not
require a new image.

## Select for character and fit

Judge the candidate in its intended role before expensive finishing. Wrong
identity, gesture or composition needs a new source or a creative edit;
compression, upscaling and edge cleanup cannot repair those failures.
Conversely, a strong image with one damaged edge deserves a local repair rather
than a fresh generation that loses everything that works.

Among candidates that pass, choose the one that best carries this role in this
placement. Finish is one quality among others: the smoothest, most complete
candidate can be the one that says least about the subject, and an awkward
pause, an uneven surface or an unidealised face can carry it better; a
polished, conventional candidate wins where polish or convention is what the
role needs. Compare gesture, light and viewpoint by what they do here. Keep
roughness that carries meaning; repair noise that only obscures the subject.
Where the role is evidence, documentation, a specimen or a real product state,
faithful neutrality is the character to choose. When an open look returns
candidates with nothing to choose between them but cleanness, revise the
direction rather than the selection; a placeholder or a job inside an
established system does not need that pass.

Selection sets the foundation for a family. Independent generations of every
pose, theme or crop can produce a different subject each time. Derive
dependent variants from a selected base where identity matters; allow
meaningful variation where roles differ. Locale can alter reading direction,
text area and composition, not merely lettering.

Compare optical rather than numeric consistency. Equal dimensions do not make
icons equally heavy, subjects equally large or padding equally balanced.
Mirroring can break handedness, asymmetrical markings, light direction, text and
semantic motion. A family should share a world without forcing every member
into the same pose.

## Repair at the scale of the defect

Choose among a local generative edit, a deterministic correction, another
candidate and a different placement. A defect is local when changing it can
preserve the rest of the composition; a cropped face may be a placement
problem, not a generation problem.

Generative edits can change details outside the region you name, and repeated
passes accumulate that drift in faces, lighting and texture. Ask for one change
per pass and restate what must stay the same. A prompt-only edit cannot
guarantee pixel-identical preservation. Where exact preservation is required,
retain those pixels from the original and composite only the approved change
through a permitted deterministic operation. Repeated patching of a
fundamentally wrong pose erodes anatomy and coherence; that source should be
rejected.

Transparency must be real alpha. Use the generator's transparent-background
option with PNG or WebP output, then inspect the decoded alpha channel at hair,
glass, shadows and edges. A checkerboard in the pixels is a painting of
transparency, not an alpha channel. If the generator has no transparent
option, a flat background that contrasts with the subject can support matte
extraction through a permitted editing route; inspect the decoded alpha the
same way.

Derivation translates the selected source into the receiving pipeline: crop,
resize, density, color space, alpha, compression, transcoding, posters and atlas
packing. Repeatable transformations keep a family consistent. Keep the source
distinct, because repeated lossy recompression and resizing destroy options for
later repair. A generative upscaler invents detail, so compare faces, text and
markings with the source rather than trusting sharper pixels.

Inspect the property each transform can damage. Alpha cleanup can leave a halo
on dark backgrounds; compression can destroy thin lines or gradients; a color
conversion can shift skin or brand color; a density change can blur small
symbols; a resize or re-encode in a tool that does not carry C2PA forward
drops or invalidates embedded Content Credentials. A video encode carries
colour tags (primaries, transfer, matrix, range) that players interpret
differently: compare the encoded file as the receiving players show it with
the master, because a decoder used only for the check can read or ignore those
tags differently. The tags describe the pixels and convert nothing: the
pixels must already be in the primaries and transfer the tags declare, and the
conversion that writes them has to use the declared matrix and range.
Correct dimensions reveal none of these.

## Motion is composition through time

Direct a shot before generating or animating it. Decide what the viewer should
understand by its end; the principal action or relationship that carries it;
how that moves, in its path, timing and spacing; at what pace against the
length of the shot; and what holds still or moves in support so that it reads.
A hold sets up an expectation, the action changes it and a reaction lets the
change register; anticipation before a move and follow-through after it give
it weight. An exchange, a piece of choreography or a crowd carries one idea
through coordinated movement, so the test is that the eye knows where to go,
not that only one thing moves. Move the camera for a reason, to follow an
action or to reveal a relationship or scale the opening frame could not show;
often a still camera is what makes a small action legible. A slow push-in or
orbit with everything drifting, swaying or breathing is a likely return when
nothing directs the shot. It suits an ambient loop whose purpose is
atmosphere, where adding an event would be the wrong edit. The directing
reference covers pace, holds, shutter and screen direction across cuts.

A still can look convincing while neighboring frames drift in identity or
geometry. A loop needs a coherent seam, not matching filenames or duration.
Entry, exit, poster-to-play transition, sound and caption synchronization
belong to the asset's experience.

Choose a shot sequence when the story needs cuts; use extension when a
continuous shot matters and the selected capability supports it. Maintain
identity and style through its supported reference or frame controls rather
than repeatedly re-describing the subject. Duration limits, first/last-frame
conditioning and whether audio is retained or regenerated depend on the
selected operation; read its contract before planning around them. Describe
each shot in those terms: framing, subject, the principal action or
relationship and its pace, setting, light, and the camera's movement or
stillness.

Watch the clip at its intended pace in its receiving context. If you cannot
play it yourself, you have not watched it: stills and contact sheets hide
stutter, flicker and rhythm. Measure what playback would show from the decoded
file: jumps, duplicated or dropped frames, and frame-to-frame change where the
image should hold still. Read its frames at their own timestamps, without rate
conversion, and compare their count and timing with the source sequence,
because extraction at a fixed rate can turn a dropped frame into a duplicate
or add duplicates the file lacks. Look at consecutive frames around the
fastest moments. Playback, frame measurements and human review are separate
observations. Report who actually watched the clip and which timing checks were
performed; under this inspection standard, keep motion uninspected until a
person has watched it. Put a short playable clip before them as soon as one
exists. A repair to
one frame can create a new discontinuity. Choose regeneration or a bounded
edit according to how much of the temporal relationship is wrong.

## Provenance and disclosure travel with the file

Generator provenance is file-specific; coverage depends on product, model,
format and date. Transformations can remove credentials or weaken watermarks.
Plan preservation and check the delivered file. No detected signal does not
establish human authorship or absence of AI editing. The provenance reference
gives signal coverage, verifier fields and signing tools.

Audience-facing disclosure is a separate decision. In the EU, Article 50(4)
requires deployers to disclose qualifying deepfakes: resemblance to a real or
plausible subject and a false appearance of authenticity or truthfulness matter
together, in the actual publication context. Photorealism alone is insufficient;
stylization or fictional intent is not a universal exemption. For qualifying
deepfakes in evidently artistic works, the manner of disclosure is reduced,
not removed. Platform rules have their own scope. The
[provenance reference](references/provenance-and-labelling.md) supplies the
classification, personal-use exclusion, verification limits and current dates.

## Finish with a deliberate selection

Inspect at the intended display size and background, then use native-resolution
detail to diagnose defects. Compare variants together. The useful decision is
select, repair or reject for this role, not a generic aesthetic score.

Package one canonical file per role with the needed variants. Keep enough
source and transformation lineage to revise the work: reference roles, selected
output, creator or generation identity, settings when available,
transformations, credentials and usage constraints. Do not promise a
reproducible seed or a rights grant that does not exist.

A single asset needs only its file and a source note. A large bundle benefits
from a manifest, because relationships otherwise become hard to retain.
Rejected candidates, contact sheets and intermediates stay out of runtime paths
unless the product uses them.
