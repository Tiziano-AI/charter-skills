# Charter · Product Strategy

A strategy is a set of choices that make one way of serving customers stronger
than the alternatives. Its parts must reinforce one another: whom to serve,
why they switch, how to reach them, what can be delivered economically, and
what not to do. A feature list has no such causal obligation.

This model earns its place when whom to serve, how to win or which bet comes
first is actually open. When the direction is already chosen, improving the
journey is `charter-product-experience` work, and a bounded trade-off without a
market thesis is answered directly with the choice and its decisive reason.
When the bet is set but the people who share the choice disagree or hold
different decision rights, `charter-decision-design` integrates their
contributions.

## Find the switching occasion

A segment is useful when its members share a need, constraints, buying behavior
and a reachable occasion. “Small businesses” rarely predicts enough. “Teams
facing a repeated reconciliation deadline with incompatible records” may reveal
urgency, current labor, buyer and route to trial.

The alternative is what happens without this product: a competitor, internal
tool, service, manual work, delay or inaction. Adoption must overcome learning,
migration, trust and procurement costs as well as price. A technically better
feature may offer too little reason to switch; a modest capability at the right
urgent occasion may offer a compelling one.

Separate user, buyer and payer when their incentives differ. A user saving time
does not establish that a budget owner can buy, nor that procurement can accept
the delivery model. A workaround can reveal a necessary workflow shape rather
than a feature to eliminate.

## Explain why this business can deliver

Differentiation is valuable when customers notice it in the outcome and the
business can sustain it. Expertise, workflow access, trust, distribution, rights
to data or a different cost structure may matter more than a visible feature.
A supposed moat needs a mechanism: what improves with use, who controls the
resource, and why a rival cannot cheaply reproduce the benefit.

Data volume alone is not that mechanism. Coverage, quality, usage rights and
feedback into better results determine whether data creates advantage or
expense. Network growth helps only if an additional participant improves value
for the relevant others; growth can also increase moderation or coordination
cost.

Connect the channel to the service model and economics. Low-priced self-serve
software cannot casually promise bespoke onboarding. Enterprise trust may
require implementation work and a longer sales cycle. Revenue less variable
delivery, inference, support and service cost gives a more useful starting point
than a gross revenue story; acquisition payback, cash timing and retention
determine whether that contribution can sustain growth.

A service-heavy wedge can be an excellent business or a deliberate path to
product knowledge. Founder labor does not disappear because it is not yet paid.
Make the labor and what will change it explicit rather than labeling the
current service software.

## Make the choices reinforce each other

Compare genuinely different ways to win, not cosmetic packages of the same
thesis. A narrow wedge can improve urgency, channel access and delivery
repeatability at once. It can also be too small or too isolated to support the
intended business. The attractive alternative deserves its strongest case.

Exclusions protect the logic of the choice. Customers, integrations, use cases
and service levels not served now are not automatically deferred commitments.
The product becomes incoherent when each exception consumes the advantage the
strategy depends on.

Sequence bets by what they make possible. A first bet can deliver a complete
customer outcome while creating trust, distribution access, data rights, cash
or a reusable capability for the next. Parallel bets that all depend on the
same scarce person are competing promises, not a portfolio.

## Let behavior discriminate

Choose observations that bear on the uncertain link. Interviews help with
language and reasons; an existing workaround or spend reveals current burden;
a paid trial shows commitment under its terms; renewal speaks to retained
value; measured channel and cohort costs speak to repeatable acquisition and
contribution economics, which none of the others proves. These are not stages
to pass in order or substitutes for one another.

Immature cohorts seldom justify precise lifetime-value estimates. Compare
scenarios when retention or service burden could reverse the choice. A useful
early bet exposes the assumption most capable of breaking the strategy without
requiring the entire business to exist first.

Recommend where to compete, the mechanism of advantage, the economic and
distribution logic, the exclusions and the next bet. Explain what would make
that choice narrower, larger or wrong.

When two wedges both look viable and their economics could decide,
[the worked contrast](references/workflow.md) shows how service burden and
cash timing can reverse a ranking by margin.
