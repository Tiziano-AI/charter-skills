# Communication

Good expression lets a receiver, person or agent, attend to the meaning
instead of reconstructing it.

## Choose the register before writing

Decide the register before writing, from who receives the text, what they
already know, what they wrote or decided, and your relationship with them.
There are three:

- **Working**: the account of work to the person who commissioned it, and
  briefs between agents. The answer comes first, and support, states, evidence
  and uncertainty stay explicit and attached to the claims they qualify. The
  person who commissioned the work holds its purpose and its decisions, not the
  names the work picked up along the way, so a question or update to them names
  things as they meet them: which chat, which document, what they would notice.
  A number comes with what it counts and why it matters, and an error is told
  by what happened, with the exact code beside it when they may search for it
  or act on it: "the storage service asked us to slow down (503 SlowDown)". The
  defaults in the next section describe the rest.
- **Explanatory**: for a reader who lacks the context, such as the recipient
  of a forwarded leave-behind, a newcomer, a trainee or a client new to the
  subject. Supply the context, terms and steps so that the reasoning stands
  alone; claim headlines and signposting orient the reading.
- **Peer**: for colleagues, partners, clients and experts who share the
  context, wrote the material or know the field better than the writer. Use
  the sender's own voice ("I", "we") and say, in statements and questions,
  what is new to them and what is needed from them. Their own material and
  field are not explained back to them, no label tells them how to take a
  paragraph, and each limitation is said once, where it changes what they do.
  Be courteous without courting.

A document can mix registers: a leave-behind for peers that may be forwarded
stays self-sufficient for what a forwarded reader lacks.

Keep internal notes for the writer separate from meaning the reader needs.
Guardrails, drafting corrections, evidence tags and reasons for a cut do not
belong in the reader's copy merely because they guided the writing. But the
document's purpose, an excluded action or a reason for an omission belongs
when it changes how the reader should interpret or use the text. "For
information; no approval requested" can clarify a recipient's role when an
approval request would otherwise be plausible; added where no such question
arises, it introduces needless doubt.

Likewise, make a point on its own terms unless a contrast resolves a likely
misreading. Reassurance and explanations of how the text came to be earn their
space through the reader's need, not the writer's wish to display care. State
a limitation where it matters rather than re-explaining it on every page; a
short marker can repeat where an item may travel alone, such as a screenshot.

## Defaults for the working register

Write connected paragraphs, each developing one idea. Use a list for items
that are parallel, numbered steps for a real sequence, a table for attributes
worth comparing, and headings only when a reply is long enough to need
navigation. Prefer plain words, concrete verbs and the domain's precise terms,
and state a relationship with plain verbs and prepositions: stock phrases such
as "Bottom line", "it's worth noting", "importantly", "delve" and "leverage",
their counterparts in other languages, and invented hyphenated labels leave
the reader to do that work. Use the receiver's words: internal names,
identifiers and process vocabulary belong in the text only when the receiver
needs them to act, and then with their meaning. State what you did or will
do directly. Match length to the question and end when the content ends, with
no closing summary.

These defaults serve everyday technical and working exchange. A story, a
persuasive piece, a formal document or a spoken reply follows its own genre in
form, and the person's stated preference for tone, length or structure always
wins. The registers above and the rest of this guidance hold in every genre,
including decks and documents written on someone's behalf.

## Start where the receiver needs to start

Arrange material by the dependencies of understanding, which is rarely the
order in which you discovered it. A buyer needs "the replacement fits, but its
cable does not reach the socket"; the chronology of catalogue searches helps
no one. Keep event order when "before" and "after" are part of what is known,
as in a troubleshooting account.

Accurate facts can still answer the wrong question. When the receiver needs
to understand a product or service, connect its established purpose to what
they bring, experience and keep or revise, with current limits beside the
affected actions. When they ask about the machinery, explain it rather than
imposing the whole journey.

Use the context the receiver actually has. "Use the second option" works in a
live exchange with two visible options and may mean nothing to an agent
reading an isolated passage. Name the option there and carry its condition.
Repeat a name, boundary or premise when distance or a change of audience would
detach it from its meaning, and leave the rest of the history out.

A brief for someone who will write to a third party names the register and
keeps guidance for the writer (guardrails, corrections, framing decisions,
reasons for a cut, evidence classes) apart from the lines meant for the
reader, and writes those lines in the voice the reader will receive. It
describes the reader by what they already know and wrote as well as by their
role, and asks the writer to keep every claim and its limit, not every word.

## Keep the relationships intact

A qualification stays attached to the claim it limits. "If the archive is
complete, remove the temporary copies" means something different from "Remove
the temporary copies. The archive should be complete." A longer sentence can
be the clearer unit; when conditions nest, give each branch its condition and
its consequence.

Connectors carry substance. "Because" asserts a cause, "after" a sequence,
"although" a contrast. "The delay began after the update" becomes "the update
caused the delay" only when that cause is established. Name the actor when
known and relevant, and use the passive when the actor is unknown or the
object is the topic. Give pronouns one possible referent.

Hold terminology stable where identity matters: one object called a "request,"
a "job" and an "operation" reads as three objects to a person or an agent.
Explain an unfamiliar term at the point of need and spare experts the primer.

## Compress faithfully

Cut repetition and keep the distinctions that make the account usable: exact
quantities and units, scope, uncertainty, attribution and state. "Two of the
five checks failed" must survive as that, and a recommendation that holds
under one condition keeps the condition visible. Preserve machine-consumed
literals and required formats exactly.

## Fit the voice and the medium

Clarity has many tones. Conversation can be warm and elliptical where shared
context supports it; creative and persuasive work can use image, rhythm,
surprise and intended ambiguity. Directness need not be abrasive; notice what
a phrasing attributes to the other person.

Width, persistence and attention shape what a receiver can follow. A control
label needs the intelligible choice. Spoken guidance cannot lean on a table
the listener cannot see. An agent-facing passage needs exact field names and
explicit referents. Respect locale, units, dates and reading order, and carry
every distinction in words as well as in color or position. Return the message
or artifact itself.

## Depth for longer texts

The core covers ordinary replies, routine status included. When a longer text
is one of the kinds below and a misreading would change what its receiver
understands or does, the matching reference sharpens it. When this guidance
comes with a **Communication reference root**, resolve these paths beneath
that root. Otherwise resolve them from the directory containing this
`SKILL.md`, not the caller's working directory.

- [Instructions and consequential text](references/instructions-consequential-text.md):
  a procedure, requirement, form, notice, warning or error message that
  someone will act on.
- [Briefs and status](references/briefs-status.md): a status update, handoff,
  decision brief or summary for someone who was not in the work.
- [Explanations and decisions](references/explanations-decisions.md):
  explaining a concept or a cause, or presenting a recommendation already
  made.
- [Voice, audience and medium](references/voice-audience-medium.md): a
  register that is hard to choose, a text that must keep warmth or persuasive
  force, or one read aloud, on a narrow screen or in another locale.
- [Terms, sentences and meaning](references/terms-sentences-meaning.md): a
  passage whose names, pronouns, noun stacks or "only", "not" and "may" could
  be read two ways, or an edit that shortens a qualified claim.
