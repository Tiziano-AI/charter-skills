# Voice, audience and medium

For a register that is hard to choose, wording that risks losing warmth,
character, persuasive force or accessibility, or a text that moves between
audiences and media. Its choices fit each text to its own reader and medium,
and choosing a register is a quick judgment about the reader, made as you
begin.

## A conversation is a relationship in progress

Natural conversation relies on shared context, turn-taking and implication.
“Yes—the blue one” can be the best answer to a visible choice. Replacing it
with a formal inventory of all options may be precise but socially and
cognitively wasteful. Once the answer must stand alone, the missing referent
matters: name the blue object rather than preserving an unusable ellipsis.

Directness and care can coexist. “This paragraph does not yet explain why
the recommendation follows” identifies a repairable problem. “This makes no
sense” turns the response toward judgment of the writer; “This is wonderful,
with just a tiny opportunity to enhance clarity” can hide the defect under
reassurance. Tone should fit the relationship and seriousness without
changing the substantive assessment.

Italian makes some social distinctions visible in small choices. “Non mi è
chiaro a quale versione ti riferisci” names the ambiguity in understanding;
“Ti sei spiegato male” assigns fault. Neither politeness nor bluntness is
inherently more truthful. Preserve the intended relationship and what is
actually known.

## The register follows the reader

The same finding reads differently to each reader. Suppose a check of a
partner's scoring method against ten synthetic sample cases found two that
fit none of its categories.

- Working, to the person who commissioned the check: “Eight of the ten
  synthetic samples score cleanly; samples 4 and 7 fit no category. No real
  case has been run, so this does not yet show how often it happens.”
- Explanatory, in a leave-behind for someone who has not seen the method:
  what the method does with a case, why a case outside every category cannot
  be scored, then the two samples, marked as synthetic.
- Peer, to the partner who wrote the method: “On our synthetic samples, 4 and
  7 fit none of your categories: 4 has no counterparty, 7 has two. Would you
  extend a category, or send such cases to manual review?”

The partner needs neither the method nor the review process described back.
The synthetic samples matter to them because they could otherwise read the
result as a finding on their own cases, so one clause says so, once.

Judge a reader by what they know, how fluent they are in the text's language
and what context they hold, not by nationality, job title or a novice or
expert label; these vary independently. A senior engineer reading in a second
language may need plain syntax and stable terms but no primer on the field.

Notes for the writer often leak into a reader's copy. A brief can rightly
tell its writer “do not ask her to redo the analysis”. If the reader has no
reason to expect more work, copying it as “Nothing here needs redoing on your
side” introduces a question the text need not raise. Asking for nothing to
be redone is enough. If the message could reasonably look like a request for
another analysis, an explicit exclusion can instead clarify the reader's role.

The same distinction governs purpose and editorial context. “This is for
information, not approval” is useful when the recipient would otherwise need
to decide whether to approve it. A reason for an omitted section belongs when
the omission would change the reader's interpretation, not merely because the
writer was told to cut it. Keep the sentence when it supplies that meaning;
leave out reassurance that addresses no plausible reader concern.

A limitation belongs where it changes what the reader does. Screenshots of
test data that may be forwarded one by one can each carry a short “test data”
marker; what the test data can and cannot show is explained once.

## Voice carries meaning beyond information

Rhythm can create emphasis, pace and feeling. A short sentence after a long
one can land a contrast. Repetition can intensify an image. “At dawn she
waited. At noon she waited. By night the chair beside her was still empty”
does not become equivalent when compressed to “She waited all day.” The
facts may be similar, but duration as experienced by the reader disappears.

Metaphor can reveal an idea through an image: “The proposal has a strong
spine but no joints” suggests structure without flexibility. In a design
critique it may need a concrete continuation: “Its sections are sound, but
none explains how the reader moves to the next.” Removing the image can
flatten the voice; leaving only the image can make the critique unusable.
The pairing serves both purposes when both matter.

Intentional ambiguity belongs to some work. A story can leave the speaker's
loyalty unresolved; an invitation can be playfully indirect. A contractual
obligation or control label has different needs. Do not erase ambiguity
merely because it exists, and do not invoke creativity to excuse accidental
confusion about a consequential action.

Persuasion may emphasize a real benefit, sharpen a contrast and create a
memorable invitation. “More room for the work you love” can fit a campaign;
it cannot replace the storage limits a buyer needs where those limits are
material. Emotional force does not license an invented claim, concealed
qualification or guarantee. The underlying work owns those boundaries;
composition keeps them intelligible alongside the appeal.

## One artifact can use more than one treatment

An opening story, a technical explanation and an instruction panel may all
belong in one document. The shift should be legible: lyrical cadence need
not enter a command, and command syntax need not drain a story of texture.
Use consistent terms for shared objects while allowing the voice to change
with each section's job. A local exception is not a need for a global style
profile or a second controlled language.

A headline compresses and invites attention; body text can supply necessary
detail. But a qualification that reverses the headline cannot be repaired
by burying it later. “Free for everyone” is not made faithful by a footnote
restricting it to current members. “Free for current members” keeps the
scope where the claim is made without requiring the headline to become the
whole terms document.

## Match the channel's affordances

Spoken text is heard in order and cannot depend on a glance back up a table.
Use audible transitions and repeat a critical referent when a listener would
otherwise lose it. “Compare the two prices: forty euros for the standard
version, sixty for the extended version” is easier to follow aloud than a
spoken sequence of column headings and cell values. A screen reader also
depends on meaningful reading order rather than visual proximity alone.

On a narrow screen, a long table can sever labels from values; a compact
sequence of labeled comparisons may preserve the relation better. In a
chart, “the red series” alone loses meaning when color is unavailable;
use the series' name as well. These choices apply to the medium the text
will actually reach.

Locale is more than translated words. `1,500` and `1.500` can represent
different quantities under different conventions; an ambiguous numeric date
can reverse day and month. Make prose understandable in its destination
locale while preserving exact data and machine-consumed literals. Explain
a fixed literal beside it when necessary rather than silently converting it.
Accessibility and precision should widen access to the same meaning, not
create a simplified account with different claims.

## Worked contrasts

- A poem's unresolved "you" may invite interpretation; an instruction's
  unresolved "it" may send someone to the wrong control. Remove accidental
  confusion and keep the expressive choice the work depends on.
- Phrasing carries attribution. "Non ho ricevuto il documento" describes a
  missing document; "Non me l'hai mandato" attributes an omission to the
  recipient. The two need not have the same support or the same social effect.
- Where layout changes meaning, use the representative rendered content
  supplied by the artifact work; source wording alone does not describe what a
  receiver sees.
