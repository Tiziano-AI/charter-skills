# Explanations and decisions

For explaining an idea or a cause, or presenting a recommendation already
made: the path that lets the receiver follow an account already established
to its conclusion.

## Build the bridge the receiver needs

An explanation connects something available to the receiver with something
not yet understood. The useful bridge may be an example, a mechanism, a
comparison, a definition or an equation. Starting with a comprehensive
taxonomy can delay the one relationship the question concerns.

For “Why does this queue grow?”, an established account might be:

> Work arrives faster than the system finishes it. Each minute leaves more
> unfinished work, so the queue grows even though processing never stops.

This explains the apparent contradiction. “The system has insufficient
throughput capacity relative to inbound volume” names the relation but may
not make it easier to understand. For an engineer comparing measured rates,
the concise rate expression may be exactly right. Expert precision and
accessible explanation are choices for different receivers, not ranks on a
ladder from bad jargon to good simplicity.

Consider this invented contrast. A component question can be answered with
“The API works; inference is not integrated.” For a receiver asking what the
product does, “The intended product turns a requirements dossier into an
assessment you can review, save and revise. The current preview shows a
prepared example; it cannot yet assess a new dossier” explains the intended
result and the current boundary. These are different answers because the
receivers need different things.

A familiar analogy should illuminate a relationship without silently
extending it. A queue resembles a line at a counter for arrival and service
rates; the analogy does not establish fairness, persistence or priority rules
in a particular system. State a limit where the receiver might otherwise
apply the analogy to the disputed property. Enumerating every dissimilarity
would destroy the bridge without improving this explanation.

## Keep explanation and evidence legible together

An explanatory sentence can sound causal even when its ingredients establish
only timing. Compare:

> Complaints fell after the new form launched.

> The new form reduced complaints by removing the confusing field.

The second adds a causal effect and a mechanism. It is appropriate only when
the supplied account supports both. If the work established a hypothesis,
compose it as one: “Removing the field may explain the fall in complaints;
the available comparison does not isolate that effect.” Do not wrap every
other fact in “perhaps” merely because this relationship is uncertain.

Distinguish a reason for a choice from a cause of an event. “We chose the
smaller room because only eight people confirmed” reports a rationale.
“Only eight people attended because we chose the smaller room” claims a
different causal direction. Reordering words for emphasis can reverse the
meaning if these relations are not preserved.

When a question contrasts two outcomes, explain the difference already
established rather than restating a category. If the account is that one
export omits formulas because that format stores values only, “The formats
have different capabilities” is true but does not expose the relevant
mechanism. “This format stores the displayed values, not the formulas that
produce them” gives the receiver a usable distinction. It need not expand
into a general lesson on file formats.

## Present a recommendation without reopening its ownership

Put the supplied choice and its decisive reason where they can be found.
Then retain the trade-off, condition or uncertainty that could change how the
receiver uses it. An option catalogue is not a substitute for expressing the
choice already made; equally, a crisp headline must not make a conditional
choice unconditional.

> Choose the train if arriving before noon remains the priority. It gets you
> there earlier, but costs more than the bus.

“The train is best” deletes the objective and trade-off. A table with every
conceivable attribute may bury them just as effectively. A comparison table
helps when receivers need to compare the same meaningful dimensions across
options; prose works better when one decisive relation carries the choice.
Never invent comparable numbers merely to complete a symmetrical table.

In Italian, “Consiglio la versione ridotta: conserva i dati necessari alla
riunione ed evita l'appendice tecnica. L'appendice resta utile a chi dovrà
verificare i calcoli” distinguishes the meeting use from the verification
use. “La versione completa è inutile” would turn a contextual recommendation
into a global judgment. Similarly, “I prefer” may express taste where
“the evidence proves” would falsely express a finding.

## Depth follows the explanatory need

A short answer can settle a familiar question. A new concept may need a
worked example, a diagram and a return to the general relationship. Put
detail where it resolves a likely confusion rather than appending a generic
primer. A receiver who asks “why again?” may need the next explanatory link,
not a synonym for the previous answer; composing that link still depends on
the underlying work having established it.

Explanation has rhythm as well as logic. Alternate an abstract relation with
a concrete consequence when it helps the reader retain both. A memorable
phrase can give an idea a handle; it becomes a liability if it replaces the
qualification the idea needs. Deliver understanding, not an account of how
many explanatory devices were used.

## Treatment within one artifact

Different parts of one artifact can need different treatment. A persuasive
opening can be vivid, its evidence precise and its procedure direct. In a
procedure, put a prerequisite before the action that depends on it and
distinguish the action from the expected result. In a status note, connect the
material change to its consequence and current state without dressing a plan
as an achievement. In a recommendation, make the chosen option intelligible
alongside the trade-off that could matter to this receiver. A heading should
identify what follows and never promise a finding the text does not contain.
