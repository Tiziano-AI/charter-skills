# Large-record analysis

Use this when a record cannot be understood faithfully in one working context,
when a study spans many sessions, or when collaborators need bounded
assignments that reduce into one account. Mapping and reducing preserve
coverage and reasoning space; they are not a fixed contributor count, provider
choice or required ceremony.

## Build a view without losing its human inputs

Establish a stable source boundary. For a growing record, analyze an identified
complete prefix and keep later additions distinguishable; an unfinished final
record is not a complete message. Keep the original with its native owner and
make only the derived views the analysis needs.

Inspect record types, identifiers, origin fields, parent links and content
variants before choosing an extractor. A role label or attachment label alone
does not establish authorship, and a queued input without a confirmed human
origin or delivery remains uncertain. Earlier audits, of this harness or
others, give a checklist of routes by which human input has arrived, to confirm
against this record rather than a schema to reuse:

- prompts queued while the agent was working, and interruptions;
- instructions passed with a compaction request;
- human text carried inside system, reminder or tool wrappers;
- messages from other sessions or agents, and scheduled or automation prompts;
- skill or command invocations that carry the person's own words;
- child handbacks that arrive encrypted, truncated or only as a summary;
- interjections stored as a separate record type;
- and the reverse: heartbeats, skill text, harness wrappers, compaction
  summaries, briefs another agent wrote to start a child or delegated session,
  and replayed or duplicated deliveries that look like the person's turns.

Useful views separate dialogue from activity. Dialogue keeps human inputs and
outward agent replies. An activity index locates tool calls, effects and
results without flooding readers with every payload. Child work and imported
reviews keep their source and relationship to the parent; omitting them narrows
claims about execution and about what children were told.

Every excerpt needs a path back to the original: source identity and native
message or row locator. Packet lines are not original source lines. For huge
rows, select a bounded field by original record identity rather than printing
the whole row. A count of parsed rows or human inputs is an extraction fact,
not a count of requests or evidence that the content was analyzed.

## Map complete coverage in bounded assignments

A named session is a complete request: partition its entire boundary, not just
search hits or episodes already suspected of friction. Account for every
original range, including quiet stretches, queued inputs and boundary context.
Distinguish content read for meaning from payloads only indexed and material
explicitly out of scope; an indexed payload that could change a conclusion
needs original inspection.

Choose assignments small enough to leave room for comparison and judgment. File
size and row count do not describe semantic density: one tool payload may dwarf
an exchange while adding little to it. Keep a request, response and
consequential action together where possible. Give boundary context when an
exchange crosses slices, while assigning one slice ownership of each occurrence
so overlap does not multiply the evidence.

A chronological map gives coverage; a question-specific lane can reconnect a
trajectory across slices, such as evolving intent or the difference between
recorded corrections and delivered results. Add a lane when it answers a
distinct question, not as a second vote on the same slice. Small records may
need no delegation. When the person asks for collaborators, honor that with
bounded assignments rather than giving every reader the whole source.

Each assignment carries:

- the question it answers and the original ranges it owns;
- the view, source locator and how to retrieve surrounding records;
- the distinction between human input, agent account, imported evidence and
  tool-observed action;
- what the return needs: requests and their changes, contrasts, successful
  cases, exact locators, qualifications and dependencies across slices.

Returns carry their evidence without a word cap, and a separate coverage
statement of the ranges read fully, read partly and not read, even when nothing
consequential was found. When the returns together would not leave the
integrator room for judgment before its context compacts, which can come well
before the model's window fills, add a reducer between them and the integrator
rather than shortening the maps.

A contributor reports an unresolved dependency rather than inferring what
happened outside its range. Assignment is not completion: reassign missing or
unfinished maps, split oversized slices, and close gaps before claiming the
coverage. Overlap cannot hide an unassigned range. Before relying on the maps,
check a random sample of their claims against the originals and report how many
disagreed. Maps quote the record, secrets included, so keep accepted maps as
private as the record and outside version control, where later work can find
them, with a pointer from the assessment rather than a standing session
registry.

A study across many sessions reads every session's dialogue in full, then reads
the remaining activity in rounds. When two consecutive rounds change no
conclusion, report that the reading has saturated, what remains unread and what
reading it would cost, and hand back. Saturation supports which patterns exist,
not how often they occur or that one never does; a claim of frequency or
absence, or a request to read every session in full, needs the complete
reading.

## Reduce relationships, not votes

Reducers combine related occurrences, reconcile intent across slice boundaries
and keep successful contrasts. Several contributors repeating one episode give
one piece of evidence; agreement is not independent when they share an
extraction or an assumption. Brief reducers and challengers with the evidence,
not the integrator's expected conclusion, and ask challengers to test both
directions.

Preserve disagreement that could change the recommendation, with its source
locations and the observation that would settle it. Keep apart an explicit
human choice and an agent's claim that it was chosen, old and effective
instructions, an intermediate result and the person's outcome.

If an extraction defect removed or misattributed human inputs, correct every
dependent map and reduction and withdraw claims built on the false absence; an
appendix of recovered prompts does not repair an analysis that already
classified them as missing decisions.

The integrator reads the contributions, goes back to the originals where a
material disagreement remains, inspects where proposed live repairs would
apply, and decides what survives. A reducer that carries only failures erases
repairs; one that carries only themes erases the ordering that tells a new
choice from an ignored existing requirement. The final coverage account says
what was read for meaning, what was only indexed, which payloads were inspected
and which histories were missing.
