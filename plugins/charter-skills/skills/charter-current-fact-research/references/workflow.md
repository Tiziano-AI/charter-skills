# Reconciling a current claim

## The proposition determines the search

A question such as "can we use this now?" compresses several possible claims:
the capability exists, the selected version supports it, the account is
eligible, the terms permit the use, and the real consumer can execute it.
Evidence for one does not fill the others. Decomposing only the consequential
dependencies avoids both a broad topic survey and a falsely complete answer.

Subject, scope, effective time and decision consequence belong together. A
regional authorization can be current yet irrelevant to the user's
jurisdiction.

## Evidence has an origin and a function

A governing text defines an obligation or contract. An implementation or direct
observation establishes behavior at a particular consumer. An analysis supplies
an interpretation whose premises still matter. These functions determine which
source can support which claim.

A regulator's current rule is stronger than a vendor blog for the obligation.
A current product observation is stronger than that rule for whether the vendor
has actually implemented it. Treating either as universally superior would
answer the wrong question.

Primary sources include laws, regulators, standard owners, registries,
datasets, adopted source and direct observations, depending on the claim.
Secondary analysis can reveal interpretation, competing evidence or a missed
dependency. Independent sources with different definitions can disagree
without contradicting each other.

## Apparent contradiction has several mechanisms

**Time lag.** A source may describe a real former state. Publication, event,
effective and observation dates help distinguish stale evidence from an error.
A cache timestamp records the cache event, not the validity of every claim.

**Scope mismatch.** Region, account, plan, audience or version can explain why
one source says yes and another no. Neither statement should be generalized
beyond its scope.

**State mismatch.** Proposed, enacted, effective and implemented are distinct.
Preview, announced and generally available are likewise distinct product states.
A forecast does not contradict an observation merely because the forecasted
change has not yet happened.

**Definition mismatch.** "Supported" may mean accepted by a specification,
covered by vendor support, or demonstrated working. Resolving the term can
settle the disagreement without more sources.

**Substantive disagreement.** After alignment, evidence may genuinely conflict.
Differences in measurement, incentives, interpretation or incomplete access are
live explanations. The useful answer exposes the unresolved proposition and
its consequence rather than picking a convenient side.

Supersession and errata can change the applicable claim without changing an old
page's wording. A fetched primary source is therefore more informative than
the search snippet that led to it, but even a primary page needs its actual
scope read.
