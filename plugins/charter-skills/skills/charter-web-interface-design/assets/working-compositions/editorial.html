A field note

Read the ground.

The rain has stopped, but the street is still moving. Water follows the joint between two paving stones, slips beneath a gate and gathers at the foot of a tree.

We tend to draw a city from above: blocks, boundaries, clean lines. A puddle offers another drawing. It shows the small depressions, the compacted earth and the places where a surface changes its mind.

At the corner, a strip of planting interrupts the pavement. Its edge is lower than the path. The difference is slight, but it gives the water somewhere to go other than onward. For a moment, the street has a pocket.

Look for the line of leaves left behind. It records a journey the water has already finished.

03 / Make room to return

Leave something open.

After the surface dries, the little garden still has a job. It breaks the hard edge of the street. It gives the tree room, the eye a resting place and the next shower somewhere to begin.

This is a way of looking before it is a design. Notice where water collects. Notice what interrupts it. Then ask whether the ground could hold a little more of what already arrives.
