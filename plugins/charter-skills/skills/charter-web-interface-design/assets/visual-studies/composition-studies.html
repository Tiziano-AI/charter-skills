Change the job: evaluate a model.

An imaging engineer needs to hold the seed, dimensions and reference constant while varying a model or sampling setting. B's persistent parameter layout now exposes the independent variables and makes successive runs comparable, as a kiln log does for firings. A's compressed settings would hide the engineer's main work.

Transfer: capability placement follows the decision being made. A creation entrance can still disclose exact controls; a model lab can still keep its output visible.

Change the job: discover a place.

A visitor exploring spaces for an informal gathering may choose atmosphere before equipment. B's pictures and independent cards now support browsing and opening one appealing room. Keep the meaningful facts, and offer aligned comparison once the visitor has a shortlist; do not turn discovery into a spreadsheet by habit.

Transfer: useful density brings related values together. It is not smaller type, more rows at any cost, or a ban on cards.

Toolbar owns placement

Flex layout, grouping, gaps and alignment live on the toolbar. Its containers do not supply control padding or stretch a button beyond its family size.

The control owns its defaults

.control declares its own size, padding, type, border and radius. It works outside this toolbar too. This group's actions use the same default density.

Content owns useful width

“Preview” stays compact; “Export image” gets the width of its label. Passive status remains unboxed and quieter, without an unused width reservation.

Changed content, same ownership.
The same flex layout wraps the action group when both regions no longer fit. Export grows for its label, retaining the family metrics. Here export needs a saved version: the pending save changes status and availability, not control size.

When a difference has a real job.

A changing zoom readout may reserve width to keep adjacent controls still. A compact variant can serve a dense table, while a touch action needs a larger target. Those differences follow content or input conditions. Here the passive status and action buttons legitimately differ; unrelated wrapper dimensions do not justify three action heights.

Transfer: align the relationship that matters. Equal heights can unify related actions; unequal widths, a quieter status or a larger touch target can be correct.

Original fictional study. The CSS shows both ownership models directly; the toolbar is kept plain so that geometry is the only subject.

Loud throughout, because the label's own printed matter is.The ground is the sleeve colour of the record now playing; the capitals are the wood type of the label's dance posters, and the name fills the measure edge to edge, as a poster printer fits a line; each release is drawn as its seven-inch label, its text set in the round about the spindle hole and its catalogue number rubber-stamped as on a pre-release; the player sits on the bottom edge like a deck. Heavy condensed capitals on a flat saturated ground also arrive whenever a page is merely asked to be bold. Here the posters and labels ask for them.

Change the job: the sleeves are the identity.

A label that releases mostly digitally, each record with its own commissioned square artwork, has made the artwork its subject. A's quiet dark ground and grid of square covers then let every sleeve speak, and B's labels in the round would hide the work the label paid for. The same holds when a label already owns A's look: that is then the direction, followed exactly.

Transfer: polish and a sound hierarchy do not make a direction. For each element, ask whether an unrelated subject would have received it.
