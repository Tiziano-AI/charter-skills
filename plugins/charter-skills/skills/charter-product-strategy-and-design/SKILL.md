# Charter · Product Strategy & Design

Follow one occasion to the result someone wants to keep. Is there a reason
to switch from what they do now? Could the product, even in expert hands,
produce the promised result from their material? If it can, where does the
person fail to reach, understand, trust or keep it? Work on the open
question. A guided tour can't teach a service to do work it doesn't do; a
missing button in an otherwise understood flow doesn't reopen the market.

## Who would switch, and can we serve them?

Before choosing a market, offer or growth bet, read
[Product strategy](product-strategy.md). Compare the occasions for buying,
not just customer categories. A family with a box of old films might buy
digitization once and want help identifying reels. A local archive may order
regularly but require a catalogue and an unaltered preservation copy as well
as a viewing file. These are hypotheses for two different offers: who can
introduce the next buyer, what work must each order pay for, and what does
this business do well enough to displace the current alternative? The higher
invoice need not be the better business if it ties up the only technician
before payment. Use the [worked economic comparison](references/workflow.md)
when labor or cash timing could reverse the choice. Recommend the offer, what
it excludes and the observation that would break its case.

## What work produces the result?

When the customer and promise are clear but the substance behind “analyze,”
“match” or “recommend” isn't, read [Functional design](functional-design.md)
before settling on components. Work backward from what a practitioner would
hand over. A conservation survey that promises a repair list must turn
observations into defects keyed to rooms; delivering a searchable album of
photos leaves the surveyor to do the survey. If the promise is a photo
inventory, that same album can be the finished result.

Work a case with enough detail to tell missing evidence from missing judgment
or missing policy. A room without a photograph hasn't been shown to be free
of defects. Assign the interpretation to someone or something equipped to
make it, exact calculations to a dependable calculation, and a spending
decision to the person who owns the budget. Use the
[worked allocation cases](references/worked-cases-and-allocation.md) when a
model call or review queue conceals that distinction. If a model already has
the criterion but never saw the relevant passage, bring the passage and the
actual input to `charter-context-engineering` to trace where it was lost.

## How does the person get value from it?

When the capability exists or is specified, read [Product experience](product-experience.md)
before deciding how someone starts, acts, recovers or returns. Design the
moment in their practice, with their words and material: a choir member can
hear their own part in Thursday's piece before naming a playlist. Next week
the useful opening is the next rehearsal's music, not a badge for returning.
The same audio player in a transcription service should resume at the
unfinished passage. If the design would suit both unchanged, read
[where product character comes from](references/character-sources.md)
before choosing what to emphasize. Keep familiar controls where both jobs
actually match.
For onboarding, engagement or an assistant, compare the
[usual designs and when they fit](references/default-kits.md) before adding
a tour, reminder or chat box. Show the actual handoff or recovery when it
decides whether the result is usable.

Choose the next observation from the difficulty:

- When people drop out, [investigate the journey](references/audit-workflow.md)
  to tell “couldn't complete it” from “completed it elsewhere.” When they
  succeed once but have little reason to return, [examine what they keep](references/product-desire-lenses.md);
  a service needed yearly may succeed precisely by asking nothing in between.
- If correcting one item makes someone lose their place every time, use the
  [interaction mechanisms](references/decision-principles.md) to choose
  whether the item or the next one should stay under their hand.
- If a desktop comparison becomes unusable on a phone or aloud, use
  [modality and attention](references/modality-heuristics.md) to decide what
  must remain visible or retrievable when people can no longer see alternatives
  side by side.

Carry the user's known action, context and result to
`charter-web-apple-and-status-interfaces` when choosing how someone sees and
controls them on a specific surface. If friction is part of *playing*, give
`charter-game-design` the player's choice and what it should feel like; a
well-placed obstacle may belong. If accounts of the underlying need describe
different jobs and the difference changes the product, take those accounts
to `charter-work-discovery` for a concrete episode that distinguishes them.
