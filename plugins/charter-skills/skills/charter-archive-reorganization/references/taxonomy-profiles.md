# Taxonomy Profiles

Use these as prompts for adaptation after selecting a primary retrieval axis. Folder numbers are
optional ordering aids, not part of the meaning.

## Intake and downloads

Useful zones:

- unreviewed intake;
- action or processing queues;
- retained reference;
- software installers or machine exports when deliberately kept;
- media awaiting import into its real library;
- review queues and movement records.

Promotion out of intake matters more than a fine-grained subject taxonomy. Define how processed
items enter their canonical collections and when old installers, duplicates, or temporary exports
are eligible for disposition.

## Personal and household records

Likely subjects include identity and civil records, finance and tax, housing and property, health,
education, employment, travel, and family. Jurisdiction, person, property, account, and year are
often secondary axes.

Preserve legal names, issue and expiry dates, document sides, translations, certifications, and
superseded-versus-current status. Highly sensitive handling and retention requirements may dictate a
separate storage boundary rather than merely another folder.

## Client or engagement archive

A typical shape begins with client or engagement, then separates commercial and legal records,
inputs, working material, reviews, deliverables, and closeout. Retrieval often depends on milestone,
delivery date, version, and recipient.

Keep the actually delivered artifact distinguishable from drafts and later edits. Shared references
should have one canonical location or an index rather than being copied into every client tree.

## Media collection

Preserve relationships among originals, sidecars, project files, proxies, selects, edits, masters,
and distribution variants. Project or event often forms the primary axis; capture date, creator,
camera, sequence, and rights are useful metadata.

Do not separate application libraries or rename files before confirming relink behavior. Visually
similar images are not exact duplicates, and exported JPEGs are not redundant RAW originals.

## Research or evidence collection

Source, acquisition date, citation identity, rights, and transformation history often matter more
than a polished subject tree. Preserve original packages and stable locators; put annotations,
extractions, OCR, translations, and summaries in explicit derivative relationships.

Subject indexes or collections may serve discovery without moving the source record out of its
provenance context.

## Project archive

Common distinctions include brief, research, source inputs, working files, review rounds, build or
production outputs, final delivery, and closeout. Keep environment-specific caches and reproducible
generated output separate from irreplaceable inputs and decisions.

If an owning application or source-control system already defines the project, archive it as a
coherent bundle instead of reorganizing its internal tree.
